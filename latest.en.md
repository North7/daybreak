### Dawn–Dusk Index

Bullish on the long view: heat sits in the late-night zone, led by cheap unrealized profit. But the weekly score turned negative 2 weeks ago and flips bearish in 2 more.

What it shows: What it is: the Dawn–Dusk Index treats a cycle as one day, from deep night (cheapest) to dusk (overheated). It looks at two things. Heat: five valuation measures (such as how much paper profit holders have, or how far price is from its long-run trend), each ranked against the last four years and averaged into 0–100, where 0 is the cheapest of those years. Direction: whether four trend measures, taken together, point up or down. Together they give five stages: heat at 10 or below is deep night (bottom zone), 80 or above is dusk (overheated); in between, a downward direction is nightfall (falling), an upward one with heat under 50 is daybreak (early rise), and 50 or more is daylight (rising). How to read it: the lower panel is weekly heat and the upper one price, both coloured by the stage at the time; circles mark past cycle tops and bottoms, with the heat then. Caveats: heat is only ranked against four years, so every cycle has a "cheapest" stretch that is not cheap in absolute terms; in the last two cycles valuations peaked before price, so dusk tends to come early; there are only about three cycles since 2013, so the statistics describe history.

Source: Cycle heat 0–100 · five valuation + four trend inputs · since 2013 · weekly · 11/24

What it shows: What it is: NUPL is paper profit or loss as a share of market cap: for every unsold coin, gains minus losses at today's price, divided by market cap. ### Power law: Bitcoin's long-run trend price

Bullish: Bitcoin is down to 0.41× its long-run trend price, below the line through past bear bottoms: on this chart that is long-run bottom territory, though price can fall further inside it and stay there a long time.

What it shows: What a power law is: plot Bitcoin's price since 2010 on a log chart and it keeps climbing along a curve that flattens over time. That curve is the power-law trend: roughly what Bitcoin is worth today if it keeps growing at its past pace, called the trend price here (each day fitted only on prices up to that day, so no hindsight). Price swings around it: bull-market tops reach several times the trend, bear-market bottoms fall to 0.24–0.48× of it. Place in the channel: past bear bottoms are joined into a bottom line and past bull tops into a top line; 0% is on the bottom line (below it also shows 0%) and 100% is at the top line, coloured from cool to hot in five bands. Multiple of trend: price divided by the trend price; 1× is exactly on trend. Caveats: the bottom and top lines join past lows and highs after the fact, and there are only four cycles. As Bitcoin grows its swings may shrink, and cheap can stay cheap for a long time.

### Sell Pressure Index and momentum quality

Bullish: Pressure 100, momentum 0.08: the bottom combination holds (day 15 at 100).

What it shows: Home-made, from daily closes only. Momentum quality is the mean ÷ standard deviation of the last 21 daily returns × √21, mapped to 0–1 with the normal distribution (drawn × 100): near 1 for a fast, steady rise, near 0 for a fast, steady fall. The Sell Pressure Index is half a slow line and half a fast one. The slow line runs on a second, shorter momentum: the exponentially weighted mean ÷ standard deviation of daily returns (span 10 days, recent days weigh more), mapped to 0–1 the same way. It adds up each day that sits below 0.45 (more the further below) and drains at the same rate when above. The fast line uses the 5-day average of each day’s move ÷ the 30-day volatility: it adds when that average is negative and drains when positive, so a big down day moves it at once. Both are capped at 0–100 and a bounce pulls them back the same day; pressure reads 100 only when both lines are there, so a one- or two-day drop does not take it to 100. Pressure at 100 with momentum quality under 0.3 is the bottom combination; cyan bands run from each one until pressure resets. Pressure at 100 only says the short-term drop has run long; for how close the cycle floor is, also read relative unrealized profit: since 2012, of the days with pressure at 100, 100% were higher a year later when it was at or below 37.5%, and only 34% when above (pressure also reaches 100 early in bear markets). The backtest has only about twenty cases; a different close source or a small parameter change shifts the counts by a few. Since 2026-11-11 the index is calculated this way, and the history was recalculated. Reading only, not a trade signal.
